# The graveyard

Three files, and this is the one that REFUSES. `RTS_OPTIMIZATION.md` ranks what is
left; `RTS_ENGINE_STUDY.md` holds the raw readings under each — including under
most of what is refused here, because a refuted proposal is very often a correct
observation with a wrong prescription.

Every optimisation idea this engine has refused, and why. Written so that the next
session does not spend a build re-proving something a previous one already disproved —
and, more often than that, does not re-propose an idea whose refutation is now the most
useful thing known about that part of the machine.

**It is not a list of bad ideas.** Most of what is here is a good idea that this engine
cannot have, and the reason it cannot is usually the interesting part. Read the reason,
not the verdict.

It has four sources, kept apart because they are not equally reliable:

1. **Refuted by MEASUREMENT** — the clock said no, on a real binary. These are facts.
2. **Refuted by a THREE-LENS PANEL** — an adversarial survey where each candidate was
   attacked independently on correctness, on architecture, and on measurement, each lens
   defaulting to refuted when uncertain. These are strong opinions with citations.
3. **Refuted by READING** — someone found the mechanism already present, or absent for a
   stated reason.
4. **The instruments that lied.** The part nobody writes down, and the one that cost the
   most time. A wrong refutation costs one idea; a wrong instrument costs every
   measurement taken with it until somebody notices.

---

## 0. The two constraints that kill most published designs

Almost everything below dies to one of these, so they come first.

**RTS IS AHEAD-OF-TIME AND DETERMINISTIC.** There is no deoptimisation, no bailout, no
OSR, no profiling, no tiering. The same source must produce the same program on every
build. Speculate-guard-deopt does not transfer, and neither does anything whose
correctness rests on *"nobody can observe when this happened"* — that is a JIT's freedom
and this compiler does not have it.

**THE COLLECTOR SCANS THE STACK CONSERVATIVELY**, finding roots in compiled frames BY BIT
PATTERN. Anything that changes which SSA values are live where — enabling a mid-end,
reordering allocations, hoisting a load past a safepoint — can silently lose a root.
`docs/engine/lost-roots.md` records a case where adding one `eprintln!` inside `collect`
changed a program's ANSWER. Failures in this class are silent and wrong, never crashes.

A third, weaker but constant: **ninety per cent of an operation is the door.** A runtime
crossing is ~15.7 ns and the Rust body of an arithmetic operator is ~1.5. So "make the
runtime function faster" is almost never the answer; "do not cross" almost always is.

## 1. Refuted by measurement — these cost a build

### Removing a Rust-side copy where the cost is a JS heap cell

**Two attempts, both refuted by the clock on 2026-09-02, and one explanation.**

`exec` builds each capture group as `subject[from..to].to_string()` and then
`Str::from_str(&text)` — two allocations and two passes. `split` does the same per
piece through `pattern::fill`. Both look like the two-answers-to-one-question this crate
refuses everywhere else.

**Attempt one**, building each group from its span instead: `exec` 860 → 880 on one row
and 1345 → 1360 on another, while the CONTROL — a string `replace` the change cannot
touch — moved 9% the other way. **Attempt two**, having `fill` MOVE the caller's buffer
into the `Str` with `owning_latin1` instead of copying it: split into 8 pieces
1200/1220 → 1195/1180, into 16 pieces 1920/1965 → 2005/1985. Flat both times.

**The explanation is in `Context::intern_value`.** Producing a JavaScript string is a
`alloc_or_die` of a region cell, a slab insert, and two field writes — a **heap cell**,
which this file prices at 40–90 ns. Measured per piece on a quiet machine: split costs
475 ns for one piece and 1200 for eight, so **~104 ns per additional piece**. The Rust
buffer is a rounding error against that.

**So the lever for `split`, `exec` and `match` is FEWER CELLS, not smaller buffers.** Any
future candidate in this area that proposes to remove a `to_string`, a `to_vec` or a
scan should be measured against the cell, and will lose. One that proposes not to
allocate a cell at all — a piece that is never read, a group nobody indexes — is a
different claim and has not been tried.

**And a measurement warning that cost real time here.** The same binary measured
735 ns for a one-piece split and, forty minutes later, 475. Every number in this section
is from an ALTERNATED run; none of the cross-run comparisons that looked convincing at
the time survived being re-run side by side.

### The lazy closure

Build a helper's closure at the first call site that refuses to substitute, instead of at
the declaration. Elegant, and it removes the need to prove that *every* call substitutes.

**Refused before it was built, on determinism.** Deferring moves WHEN an allocation
happens, and what the conservative collector sees depends on allocation order. The design
was replaced by OMISSION — the allocation has no reachable use, so it is not moved, it is
not there — which is dead-code elimination, decided entirely at compile time. That
shipped, at 241 ns → 8.7.

The lesson generalises: **"unobservable" is a JIT's word.** In a deterministic AOT
compiler the question is not whether anyone can tell, it is whether the program is the
same one.

### A second entry in the keyed property cache

A computed-key site breaks at the SECOND key, not the fourth: one key 17.00 ns, two keys
38.00. A second entry needs four words; word seven is free and words three to five *look*
free for a keyed site, because they carry the indirect form's meaning and a keyed site
never takes that path. **The lowering was written against that reading and compiled.**

They are not free. `cache::remember` shifts entry zero into words three, four and five
whenever called with `duplex`, and `cache_resolve` — which the keyed resolver delegates to
— passes true. A keyed site already has a two-entry LAYOUT cache exactly there.

And it would not have helped anyway: `remember` shifts only when the LAYOUT differs, and
one object with several keys is the same layout.

It also lands on the GC. `cache_keyed.rs` counts a root per remembered key cell and
records the failure that bought it — a site remembering a fresh cell per pass exhausted
the heap, `roots 63355 live 65396 freed 5`, over seven characters that never changed. Its
argument is *"a site remembers exactly ONE key"*. Two entries make that false.

Four entries is refused separately and for a stated reason: a read entry is three words,
the cell is eight, sixty-four bytes is one cache line, and four needs a second line **on
every access including the monomorphic majority**.

### Seven rearrangements, zero shipped

Merging the `with_current` borrow, reordering a write to remove work from the hot half,
moving a check earlier, and four more of the same shape. Every one measured flat or worse.

**The pattern is strong enough to be a rule: remove work, do not move it.** Everything
that shipped this campaign deleted something — a call, an allocation, a crossing, a
conversion. Nothing that rearranged the same work survived the clock.

### `try`/`catch` in a substitutable body

Committed with a measured 4% win, three alternations and a flat control. **The mechanism
was not in the tree**: a patch script died on its second hunk and the shell correctly
prevented the write, so only unreachable arms landed. When the mechanism was actually
built, the same shape measured 58.00 → 60.00 — worse — and was withdrawn.

Two lessons, and the second is the bigger one. A measurement can be real while the thing
it claims to measure is absent. And **a pass that silently refuses gives the same answers,
only slower**, so no correctness test can see it.

### `#[inline]` attributes on the hot Rust path

Release is `lto = "thin"` with `codegen-units = 1`. LLVM already has everything the
attribute would tell it. Struck by reading, before a build.

### A short-string inline representation

Refused because this engine READS text far more than it creates it, so the saving is on
the wrong side. Worth re-asking now that `string split 16` measures 1208 ns — but the
original reason has not been shown wrong.

### `string_const` as a `WordLoad`

Struck at 0.33 ns. The 230 sites are compile-time.

### A direct call instead of an indirect one

Measured at exactly zero: 1.154 both ways.

## 2. Refuted by the three-lens panel

An adversarial survey, sixteen angles across the four engine crates, **72 candidates**.
Each was attacked by three independent agents — one on correctness and the GC, one on the
architecture and the crates' binding rules, one on measurement — each instructed to
default to refuted when uncertain, with two votes needed to kill.

**Three survived. Forty-nine were refuted unanimously. Three were refuted on partial
votes. Seventeen were never judged at all** — their refuters died on a session limit, and
those are marked below because an unanswered question is not a refutation and must not be
read as one.

The survey's own top finding was not an optimisation: it was **nine wrong answers in
`receiver.rs`**, in code committed hours earlier. That is recorded in section 4, because
what it teaches is about method, not about that file.

### The inliner’s remaining refusals

**α-rename a spliced body's own locals, and delete the declaration-count gate that stands in for it** — refuted 2/3  
> The gate at `crates/rts-codegen/src/emit/inline.rs:254` is not doing the job the proposal thinks it is, so renaming does not replace it: `shape_of` (inline.rs:792-805) builds `bound` as ONE FLAT function-wide list — `declared_names` descends into `Block` and both `If` arms — and `closed_over`'s Ident arm (inline.rs:1209-1214) decides free-vs-bound by `bound.contains(name)` with no lexical structure, so an occurrence that lexically means a FREE binding is silently classified as the body's own local whenever the body declares that spelling in any nested block; `declarations_of(body, local) == 1` is the only thing making that approximation sound, because it guarantees the misclassified occurrence had no other binding it could have meant. Measured on `target/debug/rts.exe ir`, not argued: `const t = 100; function f(x){ { const t = 1; } return t; } console.log(f(5))` emits TWO counted calls (real call) and prints `100`, matching node, while the identical file minus the module-level `const t` emits ONE (substituted) — so line 254 is the sole refusal, and deleting it admits the first file, where the rename rewrites `t` in `statements` AND `body` including the tail `return t`, `stmt.rs:176-205` has already left the Block's scope layer, and `binding::lexical_read` falls to `globals::unbound_read` — `ReferenceError: t$0 is not defined` where the program answers `100`, with a compiler-internal spelling in a message a program can catch and read. A scope-aware rename does not save it either: the tail `t` then stays correct-by-spelling but is still absent from `Inlinable::free`, so it is never put through `free_names_proved`, never asked `ctx.flattens` (inline.rs:439-446, the check written for exactly this hazard), and never checked against `arguments` — a caller whose `t` was scalar-replaced reads `__rts$t.v` and answers where node throws. The prerequisite is therefore not a renamer at all but rebuilding `bound`/`free` lexically, which is the classification the free-name proof, the flatten check and the `arguments` refusal all read, and the proposal does not mention it. The bug report half is real and I reproduced it — a helper's `const t` spliced into a body whose own `t` is captured prints `7 6` against node's `7 5`, via omit.rs:131 → `local_candidate` (inline.rs:1519) discarding `locals` and `binding::declare` (binding.rs:232) taking the `is_captured` branch into the caller's environment slot — but symptom two did NOT reproduce in the shape I built (`const t = n|0` in the caller, `const t = y/2` in the helper answered `2.5 3`, same as node).

**A trailing `if (c) return A; else return B;` is the answer `c ? A : B`, and today it is a real call** — refuted 3/3  
> The mechanism claim checks out — counting call sites as `FuncId(n)` (see below), `guard.ts` emits 1 and both else-spellings emit 3 — but the equivalence is not exact in this emitter, and I measured the counterexample rather than reasoning it: `qz = 100; function pickq(n){ if (n<0){return qz-n;} else {return qz+n;} } function caller(a){ let qz = 7; return pickq(a)+qz; } console.log(caller(3), caller(-3));` prints `110 110` on Node and on RTS today (real call), while the fold's OWN output — the identical helper written `return n<0 ? qz-n : qz+n;` — prints `17 17` on RTS and `110 110` on Node: a silent wrong answer with exit zero, the class the honesty floor names first. The cause is the free-name proof in `candidates` (C:/Users/danie/Documents/GitHub/rts/crates/rts-codegen/src/emit/inline.rs, the `free_proved` computation ~line 226): `declarations_of(program, qz) == 1` is satisfied by the CALLER's own `let qz`, so the body is spliced and its `qz` resolves to the caller's 7 instead of the global 100 — so this arm makes every `if/else/return` helper that reads a global a new instance of a defect that today only the guard and one-expression shapes can reach. Separately the stated falsifier cannot fail as written: `rts ir` prints callee names once in a legend and call sites as `FuncId(n)`, so `grep -c __rts_call_counted` answers 1 for guard.ts, elsereturn.ts and elseblock.ts alike — I only reproduced the claimed 3-vs-1 by resolving FuncIds, which is item 14's "verify the instrument before the result". The "no new liveness shape" line is also asserted rather than checked: `choice::merge` adds a join block parameter for every CALLER binding the two arms disagree about, where there used to be one call instruction, which is squarely constraint 2's territory.

**An arrow helper's `this` IS the caller's `this` at the omit door, and only there** — refuted 3/3  
> The proposal's risk claim — "Constraint 2: unchanged — this admits a body that was already going to be spliced" — is false in the load-bearing direction: admitting a `this`-reading arrow makes `omission.uncaptured` non-empty in bodies where it is empty today, and crates/rts-codegen/src/emit/function.rs:812-813 then REPLACES `captured` wholesale with `capture::captured(body, &candidates, &omission.uncaptured)`, discarding the forced insertions above it — publications (703), `__rts_this` (711), a derived constructor's late `this` (726), the `eval`/`with` extension (756-758) — and `capture::captured` is mention-based, so the synthetic `__rts_this` is never re-added. Reproduced at HEAD with target/debug/rts.exe, the only difference between two files being one omittable helper: scratchpad `g.ts` answers 13 like Node, and `f.ts` (identical plus `const inc = (v) => v + 1`) answers 4 with exit 0, because the stranded arrow's `emit_closure_with` (function.rs:238-252) found `__rts_this` one environment further out and read the OUTER function's `this`; in the shape with no outer `__rts_this`, `a.ts` fails to compile with `EmitError::Unsupported { "`this` inside an arrow function" }` while `c.ts`, identical except the helper is read as a value, prints 8. Today that needs a coincidence — a non-`this` omittable helper standing beside a `this`-reading arrow — and the change makes it the default shape it targets: `const scale = v => v * this.k` omitted beside any second `this`-reading arrow `omit` refuses, e.g. one containing a call, which `calls_anything` refuses outright. The stated falsifier cannot see any of it: a one-arrow fixture is exactly the case that survives, since no second `this` reader is left to strand, so `rts ir` would report the crossings falling and the change would ship. Two secondary breaks: `emit_substituted` (inline.rs:469-478) documents the invariant being deleted — "one that does is only ever admitted when a receiver was proved, so `None` here cannot reach a body that would ask" — and `ctx.omits` is asked before `scope.lookup` (inline.rs:355) and is not restored across a splice while `swap_this` holds another receiver, so a `receiver.rs` candidate body spelling the omitted name (which passes `free_names_proved` whenever the program declares that spelling once) would splice the helper under the wrong `this`; also `hands_this_to_an_arrow` (659) and the declare at 1155 read the SOURCE and know nothing of the omission, so `__rts_this` is still declared, now as a plain local nothing can look up, meaning the change does not even save the slot it appears to.

**A loop with no `return` inside it, and the `i++` refusal that stands in front of it** — refuted 3/3  
> Sub-change (b) breaks the one invariant `emit_substituted` documents as its licence for splicing: it emits each body statement with `&mut super::loops::Loops::default()` — a jump stack that starts at zero — while leaving the CALLER's `ctx.finally_jumps` in place, and its own comment says why that is safe today ("A fresh `Loops` because nothing in an accepted body can jump: `straight_line` refuses every loop, label, `break`, `continue`, `return` and `try`"). Those two structures are indexed against each other: `protect.rs:257` pushes `(finally body, loops.depth())` measured in the caller's stack, and `loops.rs:468-472` runs every `finally` whose recorded depth is `> index`, where `index` is the target frame's position in the CURRENT stack — so admitting a loop plus its unlabelled `break` makes `for (const x of xs) { try { total += h(x) } finally { cleanup() } }` compute `1 > 0` and emit `cleanup()`'s body inline before a jump that never leaves the `try`, an extra `finally` execution with exit code 0; worse, `emit_jump_out` emits that owed body with the SAME fresh `Loops`, so a `break` written inside the caller's `finally` (legal: `for(…){ try{…} finally{ break } }`) resolves against the substituted loop's frame and jumps to the wrong exit block. `function.rs:865-871` is the engine's own fix for exactly this mismatch — when it switches to a fresh `Loops` for a nested function it does `std::mem::take(&mut ctx.finally_jumps)` with a comment naming the failure — and the proposal does neither the take nor the restore. Independently, the proposed loop admission is a DENYLIST ("no `Return` at any depth, no labelled break/continue") where `straight_line_at` and `declared_names` are allowlists by explicit design; it lets a `var` inside the loop body past both lists (`declared_names`'s `Declare` arm pushes every `BindingKind`; the `Var` filter lives only in `straight_line_at`), and since `function::hoist_vars` ran for the helper's own emission — which the substitution replaces — `stmt.rs:72` takes the `is_var` write path, `binding::lexical_write` finds `scope.lookup == None`, and `globals::resolves` decides between writing a global of that spelling and refusing legal code with `UnboundName`; that is the `var parent` / `window.parent` failure `hoist_vars`'s own comment records. The stated falsifier — two `rts ir` runs counting `__rts_call_counted` — is blind to all of this: the miscompiles need a `try`/`finally` in the caller inside a caller loop, or a `var`, neither of which appears in `loopbody.ts`, and the observable is an extra `finally` run or a global write, never a call count. Finally the dependency claim in (d) is wrong as stated: `candidates` refuses on locals with an unconditional `if locals.iter().any(|held| declarations_of(body, *held) != 1) { continue; }`, and `ctx.omits` relaxes only `free_proved` at the site — so candidate 1 as shipped does not unblock `for (let i…)` in any program with two loops.

**Bound emitted SIZE, because STATEMENT_BUDGET bounds nothing and the product is unbounded** — refuted 3/3  
> The candidate's load-bearing claim — "refusing a substitution is always the shape that was already correct" — is false on HEAD, because `omit.rs` makes substitution MANDATORY for a set of names decided before the body is emitted: `omit::omittable` is called at `function.rs:793`, its header states in bold that "the proof has to be COMPLETE", and every one of its clauses (the `with` scan, `read_as_value`, `captured`, the `flattened` check, `spread_calls`, the rest/default/`calls_anything` gate at `omit.rs:314-318`) exists to close one specific refusal path in `emit_substituted`; a per-caller budget adds a tenth refusal path that `omittable` structurally CANNOT close, because it runs before emission and the budget's value depends on how much of the body has already been emitted. When it fires, `stmt.rs:151` (`if ctx.omits(*name) { continue; }`) has already skipped the declaration and `function.rs:812` has already unmade the environment, so the fallback call reads a name nothing bound — and the `omit.rs` comment promising this "would say so at compile time" is STALE: `binding.rs:121-127` now routes an unplaced name through `predefined` → `globals::read` → `globals::unbound_read`, and `globals::resolves` (`globals.rs:272-278`) answers true for `PROVIDED.contains(text) || ctx.globals.contains(&name)` where `ctx.globals` is `sloppy::created` over the WHOLE module (`mod.rs:1055`) and keyed by interned spelling. So `function outer(n){ n=h1(n);…n=h20(n); const q=y=>y*2; return q(n) }` beside a sloppy `q = function(y){return y*999}` anywhere in the program calls the wrong function and exits zero — a silent wrong answer, the exact class the honesty floor names — and in the ordinary case it is a runtime `ReferenceError` in a program that runs today, from a change advertised at 0 ns. Constraint 2 I checked and concede: a refused substitution emits a real call, which is a safepoint carrying a frame, so no root is lost; constraint 1 survives literally (emission order is a function of the source) but the decision becomes non-local in a way that reaches `.stack` — RTS_OPTIMIZATION item 12 and `running.rs::an_error_says_where_it_came_from` assert two inlined frames ABSENT, and a budget makes which frames appear a function of how many unrelated substitutions preceded them in the same caller. Two secondary reads confirm the shape is not merely theoretical: `inline.rs:451-457` already emits arguments in a loop and returns `Ok(None)` on the first spread it meets, so `f(sideEffect(), ...rest)` emits `sideEffect()` and then `call.rs:88` emits the whole call again (an existing double-evaluation any late-checked budget would inherit), and the candidate's "reset per body" prescription contradicts `function.rs:840-860`, where every per-body fact is `std::mem::replace` save-and-restore — a bare reset would hand the outer body a full budget back after each nested function, so the bound would not bound.

### How far the receiver proof reaches

**`o instanceof C` is a compile-time constant when receiver.rs already decided o — the only benchmark row this angle can move** — refuted 3/3  
> Ran three counterexamples on target/debug/rts.exe at this tree, all inside the candidate's own preconditions: in `hasinst.ts` (`class Base { static [Symbol.hasInstance](x){return false;} }`, `class Derived extends Base { m(){return 1;} }`, `const d = new Derived(); d.m();`) `rts ir` shows `d.m()` already substituted (FuncId(18) appears only as a `FuncAddr`, never as a `Call`), so `d` IS a decided receiver and `Derived` IS in `ordinary` — yet `d instanceof Derived` answers **false** in both RTS and Node, because `instance_of` (crates/rts-core/src/entry/functions.rs:1274) probes `@@hasInstance` via `accessor::resolve`, which walks the WHOLE chain (crates/rts-core/src/entry/accessor.rs:312-364), and crates/rts-codegen/src/emit/class.rs:268 links `constructor.__proto__ = parent`; the `ordinary` filter at receiver.rs:100-127 inspects only the named class's own BODY, so an inherited `Symbol.hasInstance` is invisible to it and the fold would emit `true` where the engine itself answers `false` — a silent wrong answer, not a crash. Second and independent of `hasInstance`: `detach.ts` (`class Derived extends Base { detach(){ Object.setPrototypeOf(this, null); } } const d = new Derived(); d.detach(); d instanceof Base`) answers false on RTS and on Node, because `this` inside any method of any class on the chain is the receiver under a name that spells neither `o` nor `C` — so receiver.rs's stated proof ("every way of changing it has to SPELL either `o` or `C`") is simply false, and the recorded chain is not evidence that the instance's prototype chain still holds `C.prototype` at the site. Third: the fold deletes the receiver's only read at that site, so `tdzinst.ts` — where a function reaches `d instanceof B` before the `const d` runs — stops throwing `ReferenceError: Cannot access 'd' before initialization`, which both RTS and Node throw today. Separately and already live at HEAD, which the candidate would inherit and amplify: `static_methods` is keyed by NAME and inline.rs:335 accepts any `scope.lookup` hit, while `class_declarations` never counts PARAMETERS — so `class C { m(){return "class C method";} } function g(o){ return o.m(); } function h(){ const o = new C(); return o.m(); }` prints "class C method" for `g({m:()=>"the argument's own method"})` under rts and the argument's method under node (files: scratchpad/shadow.ts, hasinst.ts, detach.ts, tdzinst.ts). Constraint 2 is not the one that kills this — constraint-free ordinary JS semantics are, in three separate ways, and the design's "only `true`, never `false`" asymmetry does not help because `true` is exactly the wrong answer in all three.

**Four positions that read a receiver and provably cannot write it — `typeof o`, `o === p`, a `switch` discriminant, and a plain member read** — refuted 3/3  
> Arm 4 is unsound and I demonstrated it on the shipped release binary without editing anything: a plain member read HANDS OUT an object, and the analysis has no idea which object, so excluding the two spellings `constructor` and `__proto__` removes two of infinitely many aliases rather than making the read safe. Three probes prove it — `class C{constructor(){this.self=this}m(){return 1}} const o=new C(); o.self.m=()=>2; o.m()` and its `const s=o.self; s.m=…` variant, plus `const q=C.prototype; q.m=…` — all print 2 today and would print 1 under arm 4, silently; `rts ir` shows the mechanism exactly (`refute4/b2.ts` bare `p.d(s)` emits 0 `call_counted`, `b3.ts` adding `const q=P.prototype` and `b4.ts` adding `const q=p.self` each emit 1, and arm 4 drives both back to 0). `o.self` satisfies every stated condition — a data field, no accessor, no computed key anywhere in the chain, and a property named neither `constructor` nor `__proto__` — so no charitable reading of the arm survives, and the candidate's own EXPECTED section prices its entire win on arm 4 (`p.dist(); p.x`), which means the payoff and the unsoundness are the same clause. Worse, while checking the baseline I found the SHIPPED clause is already producing a wrong answer at e4030614: `class C{m(){return 1}} const o=new C(); const other=new C(); Object.getPrototypeOf(other).m=()=>2; o.m()` prints 1 where Node prints 2, and adding one value read of `o` restores 2 — a second instance reaches `C.prototype` without spelling `o` or `C`, so the "never read as a value" proof the candidate wants to widen does not hold today.

**An object-literal receiver — `const o = { m(x) { … } }` — whose proof is SHORTER than the class one** — refuted 3/3  
> The proposal's premise — "the proof is SHORTER; the only clause is that `o` is never read as a value" — is false twice, and I proved both live on the class path with the shipped `target/release/rts.exe`. (1) The clause it keeps is only half the guard: the other half is `declared.get(&receiver) == Some(1)` in `receiver.rs:144`, and `class_declarations` (receiver.rs:228-280) under-counts — it never counts function PARAMETERS, `catch` bindings, for-each targets, or anything declared inside an expression-position function (`StmtChild::Expr` and `StmtChild::Binding` are both `{}` arms). All four are already wrong today: `class C{m(x){return x+1}} const o=new C(); function apply(o){return o.m(2)} console.log(o.m(1), apply({m(x){return x*10}}))` prints `2 3`; Node prints `2 20`. The same with a `catch (o)`, a `for (const o of …)`, and a `const g = () => { const o = {…}; … }` print `3` where Node prints `20`. Adding one `const keep = o;` — which disables receiver.rs — restores `2 20` and `101`, so receiver.rs is unambiguously the cause. `inline::declarations_of` over-counts correctly; `receiver::class_declarations` is a second, sloppier count of the same number, which is exactly what "one source, generated views" forbids. (2) "An object literal has no second name" is wrong: `this` inside the literal's own methods IS the second name, and `value_reads` structurally cannot see it — `this` is not an `Ident`, and the Call arm (receiver.rs:329-340) exempts `o` as the receiver of `o.ANYTHING(…)`, not just of a decided method. `class C{grab(){globalThis.leak=this} m(x){return x+1}} const o=new C(); o.grab(); leak.m=x=>x+100; console.log(o.m(1))` prints `2`; Node prints `101`. So does `const alias = o.valueOf(); alias.m = x=>x+100` — `Object.prototype` is a second name reachable without spelling `o`, and on a literal `alias.m = f` overwrites the very own property being substituted rather than merely shadowing a prototype slot. The class path survives in practice only because `const o = new C()` with a never-read `C` is rare; `const log = { info(){…} }` — the proposal's own motivating shape, with a generic name that collides with a parameter, a `catch`, or a loop variable somewhere in the module — is the most common declaration in JavaScript. By the proposal's own admission it buys 0 ns on every existing benchmark row, so it pays nothing to multiply the reach of three live silent-wrong-answer bugs. Neither of the two named constraints is what kills it, and I checked: the design is compile-time and deterministic with no bailout, and its GC exposure is identical to the class path's (the receiver is read into one SSA value before `scope.enter()` in `inline.rs:464`, exactly as today). `captures_this` on `syntax::Function` does exist, so the arrow refusal is implementable; `closed_over` does refuse `SuperMember`/`SuperCall`/`New`/`NewTarget`, and `escape.rs::flattenable` refuses any literal containing a `Property::Method`, so there is no flattening interaction. The refutation is ordinary JS semantics, not the two constraints.

### How far the closure omission reaches

**A hoisted `function` DECLARATION becomes omittable, like the `const` arrow already is** — refuted 3/3

**A helper whose body contains a CALL — `omit` refuses a shape `inline` already substitutes** — refuted 3/3

**The environment a WRITING helper keeps — and the comment that explains it is wrong about this emitter** — refuted 3/3

**A DEFAULTED parameter, refused body-wide where `emit_substituted` refuses it per site** — refuted 3/3  
> REFUTED on a silent wrong answer, and it is the exact one `omit.rs` already records. `omit::assigns_anything_free` (omit.rs:200-211) walks `function.body` ONLY — it never touches `function.parameters[*].default` — and it is the sole clause deciding `Omission::uncaptured`, i.e. whether the helper's environment is withdrawn. Meanwhile `inline::shape_of` (inline.rs:831-835) runs `closed_over` over every default, and `closed_over` (inline.rs:1252-1319) ADMITS an assignment or an update to a free plain name, pushing it into `free` and returning true. **`machine_operation` bails AFTER emitting the argument: every Math call with an unproven operand runs its argument TWICE and pays 33 ns for an instruction it already has** — refuted 2/3

**`Math.min` and `Math.max` are pure computation, are the most-used Math members in the corpus, and cost 118 ns** — refuted 3/3

The candidate's falsifier is four constant ±0/NaN assertions and one `rts ir` of the hit case, none of which can observe the miss case, and the fix is not local: `FuncBuilder` has no rollback, no instruction removal and no pre-emission repr query, and `emit_call_with_name` takes expressions rather than values. **The rest of the pure numeric predicates: `Math.round` 36-41 ns, `Number.isNaN` / `isNaN` / `Number.isInteger` 32-34 ns, each one to four instructions** — refuted 3/3

## 3. Refuted earlier, by the previous survey

Fifty candidates over ten angles, thirty-seven refuted. The ones most likely to be
proposed again:

- **Turning on Cranelift's optimiser for AOT.** It changes which SSA values are live, and
  GC roots are found by bit pattern. This is the single most tempting and most dangerous
  idea on the list.
- **A direct `Inst::Call` between two emitted JS functions**, **`CallIndirect` with a
  monomorphic cache**, and **C++-style function versioning** — all three fail identically:
  `entry::called`/`invoke` maintains three per-activation stacks JavaScript observes.
  `context.callees` IS the stack `.stack` prints, `new.target` is decided by
  `depth + 1 == callees.len()`, `RunningFunction` answers `callees.last()`. **A call that
  skips them is a different language.**
- **A general DCE pass.** Refuted correctly, but for the wrong stated reason. 11 908 calls
  have unused results and 5 253 are `__rts_call_counted` — every `foo();` statement.
  Liveness was never the missing ingredient. **DCE cannot delete a call unless something
  asserts the call has no effect**, which is the effect-annotation question, still open.
- **Emitting a body twice.** `Ctx::template` deliberately mints a fresh identity per site,
  because each tagged template gets its own strings object.
- **A separate tag for strings.** The larger half was already collected by
  `Context::is_text_at`.

## 4. What nine wrong answers taught

`emit/receiver.rs` decides `o.m` at compile time when `const o = new C()` and neither name
is ever read as a value. It shipped with a header stating the premise: *every way of
changing what `o.m` reaches has to SPELL `o` or `C`, so a walk over those two names finds
them all.*

**It is false, and the survey produced nine ordinary programs that prove it.** Four came
from one cause and it is structural: **`this` inside any body of the chain is a third
spelling of the receiver**, and no walk over the name `o` can see it.

```js
class C { m() { return 1; } patch() { this.m = () => 2; } }
const o = new C(); o.patch(); o.m();      // node 2, this engine answered 1
class C { m() { return 1; } self() { return this; } }
const o = new C(); o.self().m = () => 2;  // node 2, this engine answered 1
```

The other five were shadowing the chain walk stepped past: a field, an accessor, a
computed key, a returning constructor, and the constructor itself.

Three things worth carrying forward:

**A premise stated in a header is not a proof.** That sentence read as an argument and was
treated as one, by its author, for a whole day. What made it checkable was a program, not
a re-reading.

**The adversary found it, and then refuted the fix.** The candidate proposed a repair; the
correctness lens confirmed the bug, ran the probes, and showed the repair shipped a
DIFFERENT wrong answer plus seven more of the same class. A panel that only voted would
have passed it.

**The gate for a correctness fix is the clock.** After refusing more, MEASURE: a clause
written slightly too wide turns the pass off while every correctness test stays green.
`call method` and `prop proto method call` had to stay at 4.3 and 3.0, and did.

### The tenth, and where it was hiding

A survey finds more than it reports. The nine above were one candidate's headline;
**the tenth was inside an OBJECTION to a different candidate** — a refuter, arguing that a
proposed `instanceof` fold would inherit an existing defect, named the defect in passing:

```js
class C { m() { return "class C method"; } }
function g(o) { return o.m(); }
g({ m: () => "the argument's own method" })   // rts answered the CLASS's
```

`receiver.rs` counted DECLARATIONS with a counter of its own — a class, a `const`, a
`function` — and a PARAMETER is none of those. The map is keyed by spelling, so a decided
`const o = new C()` anywhere in the module answered for every `o` in the program.

It survived the fix for the other nine, because that fix was about **what a class
declares** and this is about **what a name binds**. Two different questions that had
looked like one.

The repair was reuse, not a wider counter. `inline::declarations_of` already counts a
parameter, a `catch` binding and a loop target, and says in its own comment that
over-counting is the safe direction. **Two counters were two chances to disagree about
what a binding is**, and that is what happened. The second one is deleted.

**Read the objections, not the verdicts.** Two live defects were named in the bodies of
refutations aimed at something else. Both were checked by running; one was real.

## 5. The instruments that lied

Four in one afternoon, all of them mine, none caught by a test.

**`grep -c closure_new` over a whole IR dump counted the CALLEE LEGEND** and reported 1
where the body had no calls at all. Every IR dump ends with a legend naming every callee;
a name appears there whether or not it is called.

**An `awk` extractor with backslash escapes in a dynamically built regex** silently matched
nothing and returned 0 for every function. It printed no error.

**A `FuncId` compared between two separate IR dumps.** The numbering is per file. `FuncId(5)`
meant `c` in one dump and `__rts_add` in the other, and the comparison concluded a call had
appeared where one had been removed.

**A benchmark that reused a helper name.** `const c = …` in two functions makes
`declarations_of("c") == 2`, which kills the inliner candidacy for a reason that has
nothing to do with what is being measured. It made a real 18× win read as zero, and the
change was withdrawn as refuted. Renaming the helpers moved the SAME BINARY from 156 ns
to 8.

A fifth, from the same week and a different shape: **`cargo build --release` died with
"Acesso negado"** — the linker could not write `rts.exe` because a measurement was running
it — **and the composite command exited zero.** What caught it was comparing the binary's
timestamp to the time of the edit.

**What caught all of them was the IR and the clock DISAGREEING.** The mechanism provably
changed and the time did not move, or the time moved and the mechanism was not there.
Neither is allowed to win by default.

The rules that fall out of it:

- **Resolve ids to NAMES, inside one dump.** Never compare an index across files.
- **Give every probe unique names.** A benchmark that shares a spelling between two
  functions is measuring the name resolver, not the optimisation.
- **Check the binary is the one you built** — timestamp against edit time.
- **Run the control in the cell that has the bug.** A GC root fix was verified on a debug
  build that answers correctly with and without it, and was one command from being
  committed as a fix.
- **A count is not a clock.** Every census here — 1 079 `to_boolean` sites, 11 908
  dead-result calls, 230 `string_const` sites — is a reason to measure, never a result.
  One ranked item died at 0.33 ns.

## 6. How to use this file

Before proposing an optimisation, search it for the shape. If the idea is here:

- **Refuted by measurement** — do not re-run it unless you can name what changed in the
  engine since. Say what changed, in the proposal.
- **Refuted by the panel** — read the objection. Most are specific enough that a narrower
  version survives; several of the entries above say what that version is.
- **Never judged** — it is open. Treat it as a candidate, not as a corpse.

And add to it. A refutation is worth writing down at exactly the moment it is least
appealing to write: when the idea was yours and the clock just said no.
